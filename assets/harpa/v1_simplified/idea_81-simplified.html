legal-qa-bonito-pilot

Hypothesis

If a small language model is fine-tuned on legal QA data generated by the Bonito model from a limited corpus of legal texts, then it will show measurable improvement in legal QA tasks compared to the same model with only generic instruction tuning.

Elements

  • Independent variable: Fine-tuning a small language model on Bonito-generated legal QA data
  • Dependent variable: Performance on legal QA tasks (accuracy, F1 score)
  • Comparison groups: Same model with only generic instruction tuning
  • Baseline/control: Small language model with generic instruction tuning only
  • Context/setting: Legal question-answering tasks
  • Assumptions: The Bonito model can generate useful legal QA training data even from a limited corpus
  • Relationship type: Causation
  • Population: Small language models (e.g., T5, FLAN-T5)
  • Timeframe: Short-term experiment (1-2 months)
  • Measurement method: Accuracy and F1 score on legal QA benchmark

Description

This research explores the effectiveness of the Bonito model for generating legal question-answering training data from a small corpus of unannotated legal texts. Rather than attempting to match the performance of models trained on manually curated legal datasets, this study focuses on measuring the improvement in legal QA performance when a small language model is fine-tuned on Bonito-generated data compared to generic instruction tuning. The research will use a subset of publicly available legal documents, a simplified version of the CTGA dataset, and evaluate performance on a small legal QA benchmark.

Implementation Design

This experiment will evaluate whether the Bonito model can generate useful legal QA training data from a limited corpus of legal texts. The experiment consists of the following steps:

1. Data Collection:
- Download a small corpus of publicly available legal texts (e.g., 100-200 documents from public court opinions, legislation, or legal educational resources).
- Prepare a small legal QA test set (50-100 questions with known answers) from a separate set of legal documents or existing legal QA datasets.

2. Bonito Implementation:
- Implement a simplified version of the Bonito model that can process legal texts and generate QA pairs.
- Configure the model to use a small subset of the CTGA dataset focused on question-answering tasks.
- Process the legal corpus with the Bonito model to generate 500-1000 legal QA pairs for training.

3. Model Training:
- Select a small pre-trained language model (e.g., FLAN-T5-base) as the base model.
- Create two versions of the model:
a. Baseline: The base model with only its original generic instruction tuning.
b. Bonito-enhanced: The base model fine-tuned on the Bonito-generated legal QA dataset.
- Fine-tune the Bonito-enhanced model using standard fine-tuning procedures with appropriate learning rate and batch size for the small model.

4. Evaluation:
- Evaluate both models on the legal QA test set.
- Calculate accuracy (percentage of correctly answered questions) and F1 score for both models.
- Perform error analysis to identify types of questions where the Bonito-enhanced model performs better or worse.
- Use bootstrap resampling to determine statistical significance of any performance differences.

5. Output and Analysis:
- Save the fine-tuned model weights for future use.
- Generate a report with tables and charts showing:
a. Overall accuracy and F1 scores for both models.
b. Performance breakdown by question type or legal domain.
c. Examples of questions where the Bonito-enhanced model improved or degraded performance.
d. Statistical significance of the performance difference.
- Analyze the results to determine whether the Bonito-generated data provided meaningful improvement and under what conditions.

The experiment should be implemented using Python with the Hugging Face Transformers library for model training and evaluation. All code should be modular and well-documented to facilitate future extensions of the research.
