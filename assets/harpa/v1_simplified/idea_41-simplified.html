ID: idea_41-simplified

simple-temporal-smoothing-diffusion

Hypothesis

Applying Temporal Latent Smoothing to the latent representations in a basic video diffusion model will significantly improve temporal consistency in short video generation compared to the same model without smoothing, while maintaining comparable visual quality.

Elements

  • Independent variable: Application of Temporal Latent Smoothing techniques (with different smoothing functions)
  • Dependent variable: ['Temporal consistency score', 'Visual quality score', 'Inference time']
  • Comparison groups: ['Basic video diffusion model without smoothing', 'Basic video diffusion model with moving average smoothing', 'Basic video diffusion model with Gaussian smoothing']
  • Baseline/control: Basic video diffusion model without smoothing
  • Context/setting: Short video generation (5-10 seconds)
  • Assumptions: ['The smoothing will have a measurable impact on temporal consistency', 'The computational overhead of smoothing is minimal compared to the diffusion process']
  • Relationship type: Causation
  • Population: Short synthetic videos
  • Timeframe: Not specified
  • Measurement method: Quantitative metrics and qualitative assessment

Description

This research explores the application of Temporal Latent Smoothing to improve short video generation using a basic diffusion model. The project focuses on implementing a simple smoothing technique that operates on the latent representations during the diffusion process to enhance temporal consistency between frames. Rather than tackling extremely long videos with complex hierarchical architectures, this research targets short video clips (5-10 seconds) and examines how even basic temporal smoothing can significantly improve visual coherence. The study will implement and compare different smoothing functions (e.g., moving average, Gaussian smoothing) to determine which approach yields the best balance of temporal consistency and computational efficiency. This simplified approach provides an accessible entry point to video diffusion research while still offering potential insights into temporal coherence challenges.
