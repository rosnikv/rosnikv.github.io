Title

CausalPhenoNet: Discovering Causal Phenotype-Disease Relationships from Heterogeneous EHR Data

Problem Statement

Identifying causal relationships between phenotypes and diseases from heterogeneous electronic health record (EHR) data remains challenging due to confounding factors, selection bias, and the complex nature of medical data. Current approaches often rely on statistical associations or simple causal inference methods that struggle with the high-dimensional, noisy nature of EHR data.

Motivation

Existing methods for causal discovery in EHR data often fail to capture the complex, multi-modal nature of medical information and struggle with the high dimensionality and noise inherent in such datasets. By combining advanced causal inference techniques with deep learning and natural language processing, we can uncover more robust and interpretable causal relationships between phenotypes and diseases. This approach leverages the strengths of large language models (LLMs) in processing unstructured text data and integrates it with structured EHR data and medical imaging, potentially leading to more accurate and clinically relevant discoveries.

Proposed Method

We propose CausalPhenoNet, a novel framework for discovering causal phenotype-disease relationships from heterogeneous EHR data. Our approach consists of three main components: 1) A multi-modal encoder that processes structured EHR data, free-text clinical notes, and medical imaging data to create a unified patient representation. 2) A causal discovery module that leverages recent advances in neural causal discovery to identify potential causal relationships between phenotypes and diseases. 3) A counterfactual reasoning module that generates hypothetical patient trajectories under different interventions, allowing for more robust causal inferences.

Step-by-Step Experiment Plan

Step 1: Data Preparation

Obtain a large-scale EHR dataset containing structured data (e.g., lab results, diagnoses), unstructured clinical notes, and medical imaging data. Preprocess the data to handle missing values, normalize numerical features, and tokenize text data. Split the dataset into training, validation, and test sets.

Step 2: Implement Multi-modal Encoder

Develop a multi-modal encoder using transformer models for text (e.g., BERT), convolutional neural networks for images, and graph neural networks for structured data. Train this encoder on the preprocessed EHR data to create unified patient representations.

Step 3: Implement Causal Discovery Module

Implement a neural causal discovery module based on recent advances such as Neural ODEs or NOTEARS. Incorporate domain knowledge from phenotype ontologies as prior information to guide the causal discovery process.

Step 4: Implement Counterfactual Reasoning Module

Develop a variational autoencoder-based module for generating counterfactual patient trajectories. Train this module on the encoded patient representations.

Step 5: Integration and Training

Integrate the three components into the CausalPhenoNet framework. Implement a multi-task learning approach that jointly optimizes for causal discovery, phenotype prediction, and disease progression modeling. Train the entire framework end-to-end on the training dataset.

Step 6: Evaluation

Evaluate CausalPhenoNet on the test set, focusing on complex diseases with known genetic and environmental risk factors. Compare against existing causal discovery methods, including PC algorithm, FCI, and recent neural causal discovery approaches. Use metrics such as accuracy of discovered causal relationships (validated against known biological mechanisms and clinical trials), ability to predict treatment effects, and interpretability of the discovered causal structures.

Step 7: Analysis and Interpretation

Analyze the discovered causal relationships and generate visualizations of the causal graphs. Collaborate with clinical experts to interpret the results and assess their potential impact on clinical decision-making.

Step 8: Ablation Studies

Conduct ablation studies to assess the contribution of each component (multi-modal encoder, causal discovery module, counterfactual reasoning module) to the overall performance of CausalPhenoNet.

Step 9: Robustness and Sensitivity Analysis

Perform robustness checks and sensitivity analyses to assess the stability of the discovered causal relationships under different data perturbations and model hyperparameters.

Test Case Examples

Baseline Prompt Input

Given the patient's EHR data, identify potential causal relationships between observed phenotypes and diagnosed diseases.

Baseline Prompt Expected Output

Based on statistical associations in the EHR data, we observe a strong correlation between elevated blood pressure (phenotype) and diagnosis of hypertension (disease). However, we cannot conclude causality from this association alone.

Proposed Prompt Input

Using CausalPhenoNet, analyze the patient's multi-modal EHR data to identify causal relationships between phenotypes and diseases, considering potential confounders and generating counterfactual scenarios.

Proposed Prompt Expected Output

CausalPhenoNet analysis reveals a causal relationship between sustained elevated blood pressure (phenotype) and development of hypertension (disease). The model accounts for potential confounders such as age, BMI, and family history. Counterfactual analysis suggests that interventions to lower blood pressure could reduce the risk of hypertension by 30%. The causal graph also indicates that elevated blood pressure causally influences the development of other cardiovascular diseases, with hypertension acting as a mediator.

Explanation

CausalPhenoNet provides a more comprehensive and robust analysis of causal relationships by integrating multi-modal data, accounting for confounders, and generating counterfactual scenarios. This allows for more actionable insights and a deeper understanding of the causal pathways between phenotypes and diseases.

Fallback Plan

If CausalPhenoNet does not achieve the expected performance improvements, we can pivot the project in several ways. First, we can conduct a detailed error analysis to identify which components of the model are underperforming. This could lead to insights about the limitations of current causal discovery methods in healthcare settings. Second, we can focus on improving the interpretability of the model, even if its predictive performance is not superior to baselines. This could involve developing novel visualization techniques for multi-modal causal graphs or creating natural language explanations for the discovered causal relationships. Third, we could shift our focus to a specific disease area where our approach shows the most promise, conducting a deep dive into the causal mechanisms for that particular condition. Finally, we could explore the integration of external knowledge bases or literature-derived causal relationships to enhance the model's performance and provide a comparison between data-driven and knowledge-based causal discovery in healthcare.
