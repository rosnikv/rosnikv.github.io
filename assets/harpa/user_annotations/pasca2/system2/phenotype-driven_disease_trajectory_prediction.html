Title

PhenNet: A Deep Learning Architecture for Personalized Disease Progression Modeling Using Phenotype Ontologies and Electronic Health Records

Problem Statement

Current disease progression models often fail to capture the complex, non-linear relationships between phenotypes and disease trajectories, leading to inaccurate predictions and suboptimal treatment planning. Traditional approaches rely on linear models or simple machine learning algorithms that struggle to model the intricate interactions between multiple phenotypes over time. This limitation hinders the development of personalized medicine and effective disease management strategies.

Motivation

By leveraging advanced deep learning architectures and incorporating rich phenotypic data from EHRs and ontologies, we can create more accurate and personalized disease progression models. Existing methods often overlook the hierarchical structure of phenotype ontologies and the temporal dynamics of disease progression. Our proposed PhenNet architecture aims to address these limitations by combining graph neural networks (GNNs) and transformer models to capture both the ontological relationships between phenotypes and the complex temporal patterns in patient data.

Proposed Method

PhenNet is a novel architecture that combines graph neural networks (GNNs) and transformer models to capture both the hierarchical structure of phenotype ontologies and the temporal dynamics of disease progression. The method consists of the following steps:
1. Phenotype Ontology Encoding: Encode the phenotype ontology as a graph, with nodes representing phenotypes and edges representing relationships.
2. GNN Processing: Use a GNN to process the ontology graph and generate phenotype embeddings that capture the ontological structure.
3. EHR Data Processing: Process the patient's longitudinal EHR data using a transformer encoder, incorporating the phenotype embeddings as additional input features.
4. Prediction: Use a decoder network to predict future disease states and potential complications.
5. Multi-task Learning: Incorporate a multi-task learning approach to simultaneously predict missing phenotypes and future disease states.
6. Attention Mechanism: Implement a novel attention mechanism that allows the model to focus on the most relevant phenotypes for each prediction task.

Step-by-Step Experiment Plan

Step 1: Data Preparation

1.1. Obtain large-scale EHR datasets focusing on complex chronic diseases such as diabetes and cardiovascular disease.
1.2. Preprocess the EHR data, including data cleaning, normalization, and encoding of clinical events and diagnoses.
1.3. Obtain and preprocess the relevant phenotype ontology (e.g., Human Phenotype Ontology) for the selected diseases.
1.4. Split the data into training, validation, and test sets, ensuring temporal consistency.

Step 2: Model Implementation

2.1. Implement the GNN component for processing the phenotype ontology graph using a library such as PyTorch Geometric.
2.2. Implement the transformer encoder for processing longitudinal EHR data using a library such as Hugging Face Transformers.
2.3. Implement the decoder network for predicting future disease states and complications.
2.4. Implement the multi-task learning component for predicting missing phenotypes and future disease states.
2.5. Implement the novel attention mechanism for focusing on relevant phenotypes.

Step 3: Training

3.1. Define the loss functions for the multi-task learning objectives.
3.2. Implement the training loop, including batching of graph and sequence data.
3.3. Train the model on the prepared dataset, using the validation set for hyperparameter tuning and early stopping.

Step 4: Evaluation

4.1. Evaluate PhenNet on the test set using the following metrics:
- Prediction accuracy for future disease states
- Time-to-event prediction for major complications
- Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic curve (AUROC) for binary outcomes
- Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for continuous outcomes
4.2. Compare PhenNet's performance against the following baselines:
- Traditional time-series models (e.g., ARIMA)
- Standard machine learning approaches (e.g., random forests)
- Simpler deep learning models (e.g., LSTMs)
4.3. Conduct ablation studies to assess the impact of different components of PhenNet, such as the GNN-based phenotype encoder and the multi-task learning approach.

Step 5: Analysis and Interpretation

5.1. Analyze the attention weights to identify the most relevant phenotypes for different prediction tasks.
5.2. Visualize the learned phenotype embeddings to understand how the model captures the ontological structure.
5.3. Generate case studies demonstrating how PhenNet's predictions differ from baseline models for specific patients.
5.4. Analyze the model's performance across different subgroups of patients to assess its generalizability and potential biases.

Test Case Examples

Baseline Model Input

Patient history: 55-year-old male, diagnosed with type 2 diabetes 5 years ago, hypertension for 3 years, and obesity (BMI 32). Recent lab results show HbA1c of 7.8% and LDL cholesterol of 130 mg/dL.

Baseline Model Expected Output

Predicted 5-year risk of cardiovascular event: 15%

Baseline Model Explanation

The baseline model (e.g., logistic regression) makes a simple prediction based on a few risk factors without considering the complex interactions between phenotypes or the temporal progression of the disease.

PhenNet Input

Patient history: 55-year-old male, diagnosed with type 2 diabetes 5 years ago, hypertension for 3 years, and obesity (BMI 32). Recent lab results show HbA1c of 7.8% and LDL cholesterol of 130 mg/dL. Additional EHR data includes medication history, lifestyle factors, and family history of cardiovascular disease.

PhenNet Expected Output

Predicted 5-year risk of cardiovascular event: 22%
Top contributing factors:
1. Uncontrolled diabetes (HbA1c trend)
2. Family history of cardiovascular disease
3. Obesity progression
Recommended interventions:
1. Intensify diabetes management
2. Initiate statin therapy
3. Lifestyle modification program

PhenNet Explanation

PhenNet provides a more accurate risk prediction by considering the complex interactions between multiple phenotypes, their temporal progression, and additional factors from the EHR. It also offers interpretable insights into the contributing factors and suggests personalized interventions.

Fallback Plan

If PhenNet does not achieve the expected performance improvements, we will conduct a thorough analysis to understand the limitations and potential areas for improvement. This may include: 1) Analyzing the learned phenotype embeddings to ensure they capture meaningful relationships from the ontology. If not, we may need to explore alternative GNN architectures or pre-training strategies. 2) Investigating the temporal modeling component by visualizing attention patterns and comparing them to clinical expertise. We may consider incorporating domain knowledge more explicitly if the model fails to capture important temporal dependencies. 3) Examining cases where PhenNet performs worse than baseline models to identify potential weaknesses or biases in the approach. 4) Exploring the impact of data quality and quantity on model performance, potentially leading to recommendations for improved data collection or curation practices in clinical settings. 5) Considering the integration of external knowledge bases or literature-derived features to supplement the information available in EHRs and ontologies. These analyses could form the basis of a comprehensive study on the challenges and opportunities in applying deep learning to personalized disease progression modeling, even if the original performance goals are not met.
