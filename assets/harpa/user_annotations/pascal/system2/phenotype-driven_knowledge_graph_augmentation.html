Title

Phenotype-Driven Knowledge Graph Augmentation for Improved Few-Shot Rare Disease Diagnosis

Problem Statement

Current approaches for rare disease diagnosis using large language models (LLMs) often struggle with limited training data and incomplete knowledge graphs, leading to inaccurate or inconsistent diagnoses. This problem is particularly acute for rare diseases, where the scarcity of data and the complexity of phenotype-disease relationships pose significant challenges.

Motivation

Existing methods typically rely on static knowledge graphs or pre-defined ontologies, which may not capture the full spectrum of rare disease phenotypes and their relationships. By dynamically augmenting knowledge graphs with phenotype information extracted from medical literature and clinical notes, we can create a more comprehensive and up-to-date knowledge base for rare disease diagnosis. This approach leverages the strengths of LLMs in natural language understanding and generation, while addressing their limitations in maintaining consistent and accurate factual knowledge.

Proposed Method

We introduce Phenotype-Driven Knowledge Graph Augmentation (PDKGA), a novel approach that uses LLMs to extract phenotype information from unstructured medical texts and automatically incorporates it into existing knowledge graphs. The process involves four main steps: 1) Using LLMs to identify and extract novel phenotype descriptions from medical literature and clinical notes. 2) Employing a graph neural network to predict potential connections between newly extracted phenotypes and existing entities in the knowledge graph. 3) Utilizing a reinforcement learning framework to optimize the graph augmentation process, rewarding actions that lead to improved diagnostic accuracy. 4) Implementing a continuous learning mechanism that updates the knowledge graph in real-time as new information becomes available.

Step-by-Step Experiment Plan

Step 1: Data Collection and Preprocessing

Gather datasets from RareBench and MyGene2. Preprocess the data to extract relevant text for phenotype extraction. Split the data into training, validation, and test sets.

Step 2: Baseline Model Implementation

Implement baseline models including standard few-shot prompting and chain-of-thought prompting for rare disease diagnosis using GPT-4 API. Evaluate their performance on the test set.

Step 3: Phenotype Extraction

Fine-tune a smaller open-source LLM (e.g., BERT) on a subset of the data for phenotype extraction. Use GPT-4 to generate synthetic labeled data for fine-tuning. Evaluate the extraction performance using precision, recall, and F1-score.

Step 4: Knowledge Graph Construction

Implement a graph neural network (GNN) using PyTorch Geometric. Train the GNN to predict connections between extracted phenotypes and existing entities in the knowledge graph. Use GPT-4 to validate a subset of the predicted connections.

Step 5: Reinforcement Learning Framework

Implement a reinforcement learning agent using the Stable Baselines3 library. Define the reward function based on diagnostic accuracy improvements. Train the agent to optimize the graph augmentation process.

Step 6: Continuous Learning Mechanism

Develop a pipeline for continuous updates to the knowledge graph. Implement a scheduler that periodically triggers the update process based on new incoming data.

Step 7: PDKGA Implementation

Integrate all components into the PDKGA system. Implement the few-shot prompting mechanism that leverages the augmented knowledge graph for rare disease diagnosis.

Step 8: Evaluation

Evaluate PDKGA against baselines on the test set. Measure diagnostic accuracy, F1-score for phenotype extraction, and graph quality metrics (clustering coefficient, average path length). Conduct ablation studies to assess the impact of each component.

Step 9: Longitudinal Study

Set up a simulated environment for a longitudinal study. Gradually introduce new data over time and evaluate the system's ability to incorporate new knowledge and improve diagnostic accuracy.

Step 10: Analysis and Reporting

Analyze the results, including error analysis and performance breakdown by disease categories. Prepare visualizations of the augmented knowledge graph and performance trends over time.

Test Case Examples

Baseline Prompt Input (Direct Prompting)

Patient presents with recurrent bone fractures, blue sclera, and hearing loss. What is the most likely diagnosis?

Baseline Prompt Expected Output (Direct Prompting)

Based on the symptoms provided, the most likely diagnosis is Osteogenesis Imperfecta (OI), also known as brittle bone disease.

Proposed Prompt Input (PDKGA)

Given the augmented knowledge graph and the following patient presentation, what is the most likely diagnosis? Patient presents with recurrent bone fractures, blue sclera, and hearing loss.

Proposed Prompt Expected Output (PDKGA)

Based on the augmented knowledge graph and the patient's symptoms, the most likely diagnosis is Osteogenesis Imperfecta (OI), Type I. The knowledge graph indicates a strong association between blue sclera, recurrent fractures, and hearing loss with OI Type I. Additionally, recent literature extracted into the graph suggests that certain COL1A1 gene mutations are specifically linked to this phenotype cluster, further supporting the diagnosis of OI Type I over other OI subtypes or similar conditions like Ehlers-Danlos syndrome.

Explanation

The PDKGA approach provides a more detailed and confident diagnosis by leveraging the augmented knowledge graph, which includes recent research findings and specific genetic associations. This allows for a more precise subtyping of the condition, which is crucial for appropriate management and genetic counseling.

Fallback Plan

If the proposed PDKGA method does not significantly improve diagnostic accuracy, we will pivot to an analysis of the knowledge graph augmentation process. We will investigate the quality and relevance of the extracted phenotypes, the accuracy of the predicted connections in the graph, and the effectiveness of the reinforcement learning optimization. This analysis could provide valuable insights into the challenges of integrating unstructured medical text into structured knowledge graphs. Additionally, we will explore alternative approaches such as meta-learning for few-shot diagnosis or multi-task learning that combines diagnosis with phenotype extraction. These investigations could lead to a paper focused on the limitations and potential improvements of knowledge graph-based approaches in rare disease diagnosis.
