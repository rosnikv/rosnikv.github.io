Motivation

The source paper is "Scideator: Human-LLM Scientific Idea Generation Grounded in Research-Paper Facet Recombination" (18 citations, 2024, ID: 1343dedea56bbf3ba48d0971aee177b5add61105). This idea builds on a progression of related work [92c82a51ad13c361d052987694cf93d6a72d5789, fc7e58340e84edf85023cac2894c51921ca8c501, fccde18f25977d129af241996a769bdb23d1148d].

The analysis reveals a progression from using LLMs for idea generation (source paper) to automating the entire research process (Paper 0) and improving peer review efficiency (Paper 2). However, a gap remains in optimizing the ideation process itself, particularly in evaluating the novelty and potential impact of generated ideas. Building upon the existing work, a research idea can focus on enhancing the Scideator tool by integrating a dynamic feedback mechanism that iteratively refines generated ideas based on real-time novelty and impact assessments. This would address the limitation of static idea evaluation and further improve the ideation process.

Hypothesis

Integrating LLM-Enhanced Instant Feedback with Dynamic Feedback Mechanisms in Co-Creation will significantly improve the originality and potential impact scores of ideas generated in the Scideator tool compared to static, one-time evaluation methods.

Research Gap

Existing studies have not explored the impact of combining LLM-Enhanced Instant Feedback with Dynamic Feedback Mechanisms in Co-Creation on originality and potential impact scores. This hypothesis addresses the gap by testing this novel combination in a real-time feedback environment.

Hypothesis Elements

Independent variable: Integration of LLM-Enhanced Instant Feedback with Dynamic Feedback Mechanisms in Co-Creation

Dependent variable: Originality and potential impact scores of ideas

Comparison groups: Three conditions: Baseline Condition (static, one-time evaluation), LLM-Only Condition (only LLM-Enhanced Instant Feedback), and Integrated Condition (both LLM-Enhanced Instant Feedback and Dynamic Feedback Mechanisms)

Baseline/control: Static, one-time evaluation methods with no real-time feedback

Context/setting: Scideator tool for idea generation

Assumptions: Real-time feedback can influence idea quality; LLMs can provide meaningful creative feedback; Dynamic facilitation can enhance group ideation processes; Originality and impact can be objectively measured

Relationship type: Causal (the integration 'will significantly improve' the scores)

Population: Participants using the Scideator tool for idea generation

Timeframe: Real-time during idea generation sessions, with evaluation occurring after idea completion

Measurement method: Originality and potential impact scores (0-10 scale) assessed by both LLM evaluation and human raters who are blind to the experimental condition

Overview

This research explores the integration of LLM-Enhanced Instant Feedback with Dynamic Feedback Mechanisms in Co-Creation within the Scideator tool to enhance idea originality and potential impact scores. LLM-Enhanced Instant Feedback uses Large Language Models to provide personalized, real-time feedback during idea generation, fostering creativity and improving information retrieval. Dynamic Feedback Mechanisms in Co-Creation adjust facilitation approaches in real-time based on group needs, maintaining diversity and cohesion in idea generation. By combining these two feedback mechanisms, the hypothesis aims to create a more interactive and responsive ideation environment. This approach addresses gaps in existing research by testing a novel feedback combination that has not been extensively explored. The expected outcome is that this integration will lead to higher originality and potential impact scores compared to static evaluation methods. The Scideator tool is chosen for its ability to support dynamic, real-time feedback, making it an ideal platform for testing this hypothesis.

Background

LLM-Enhanced Instant Feedback: This variable represents the use of Large Language Models to provide real-time, personalized feedback during the idea generation process. It is implemented by analyzing user inputs and offering constructive suggestions or critiques. The feedback is immediate and tailored to the individual's creative process, enhancing both individual and group performance. This implementation is selected for its high confidence and proven ability to nurture creativity and improve information retrieval effectiveness.

Dynamic Feedback Mechanisms in Co-Creation: This variable involves using dynamic feedback mechanisms to adjust facilitation approaches in real-time based on group needs. The system monitors group progress and provides feedback to facilitators, allowing them to make adjustments to maintain diversity and cohesion in idea generation. This approach is chosen for its ability to enhance the creative process by providing timely and relevant input to refine ideas. It is particularly relevant for large-scale, distributed idea generation settings.

Implementation

The hypothesis will be implemented by integrating LLM-Enhanced Instant Feedback and Dynamic Feedback Mechanisms in Co-Creation within the Scideator tool. The LLM-Enhanced Instant Feedback will be configured to analyze user inputs and provide real-time suggestions or critiques. The Dynamic Feedback Mechanisms will monitor group progress and provide feedback to facilitators, allowing for adjustments in facilitation strategies. The integration will occur at the data and control-flow level, with the LLM feedback influencing the dynamic adjustments made by the facilitation system. The system will be set up to provide feedback on originality and potential impact, guiding users in refining their ideas. The implementation will involve setting up a feedback loop where user inputs are continuously analyzed, and facilitators receive real-time updates on group progress. The hypothesis will be tested using a series of idea generation sessions within the Scideator tool, comparing the originality and potential impact scores of ideas generated with the integrated feedback mechanisms to those generated using static evaluation methods.

Operationalization Information

Please build an experiment to test the hypothesis that integrating LLM-Enhanced Instant Feedback with Dynamic Feedback Mechanisms in Co-Creation will significantly improve the originality and potential impact scores of ideas generated in the Scideator tool compared to static, one-time evaluation methods.

Experiment Overview

This experiment will compare three conditions in the Scideator tool:
1. Baseline Condition: Static, one-time evaluation of ideas with no real-time feedback
2. LLM-Only Condition: Ideas receive only LLM-Enhanced Instant Feedback during generation
3. Integrated Condition: Ideas receive both LLM-Enhanced Instant Feedback and Dynamic Feedback Mechanisms during generation

Implementation Details

Pilot Mode Configuration

Implement a global variable PILOT_MODE with three possible settings: MINI_PILOT, PILOT, or FULL_EXPERIMENT. The experiment should start with MINI_PILOT mode, then proceed to PILOT if successful, but stop before FULL_EXPERIMENT for human verification.

  • MINI_PILOT: Use 3 participants per condition, with each participant generating 2 ideas. Total: 18 ideas across all conditions.
  • PILOT: Use 10 participants per condition, with each participant generating 5 ideas. Total: 150 ideas across all conditions.
  • FULL_EXPERIMENT: Use 30 participants per condition, with each participant generating 10 ideas. Total: 900 ideas across all conditions.

Scideator Tool Configuration

Configure the Scideator tool to support all three experimental conditions. Each condition should have the same interface and idea submission process, differing only in the feedback mechanisms.

LLM-Enhanced Instant Feedback Module

Implement the LLM-Enhanced Instant Feedback module to provide real-time feedback on ideas as they are being typed. The feedback should focus on:
1. Originality enhancement suggestions
2. Impact potential improvements
3. Clarity and completeness recommendations

The LLM should use GPT-4 or an equivalent model and should be prompted to provide constructive, specific feedback that enhances creativity without being overly prescriptive. The feedback should appear in a sidebar next to the idea input field and update every 10 seconds or when the user pauses typing for more than 3 seconds.

Dynamic Feedback Mechanisms Module

Implement the Dynamic Feedback Mechanisms module to:
1. Monitor group progress in real-time
2. Analyze patterns in idea generation across participants
3. Provide adaptive facilitation suggestions based on:
- Diversity of ideas being generated
- Depth vs. breadth of exploration
- Potential for cross-pollination between ideas
- Identification of promising but underdeveloped concepts

This module should maintain a dashboard for facilitators showing:
- Real-time metrics on idea diversity and quality
- Suggestions for facilitation interventions
- Alerts when the group appears stuck or converging prematurely

The module should update every 30 seconds and provide specific, actionable recommendations for facilitators.

Integration of Feedback Mechanisms

For the Integrated Condition, implement a feedback loop where:
1. The LLM-Enhanced Instant Feedback analyzes individual ideas
2. The Dynamic Feedback Mechanisms module receives this analysis
3. The Dynamic module aggregates individual feedback to inform group-level facilitation
4. Facilitators receive suggestions that balance individual creativity with group cohesion

Evaluation Metrics

Implement evaluation of ideas using two primary metrics:

  1. Originality Score (0-10): Assessed by:
  2. LLM evaluation of novelty compared to existing ideas in the database
  3. Uniqueness of approach or combination of concepts
  4. Deviation from conventional thinking in the domain

  1. Potential Impact Score (0-10): Assessed by:
  2. Feasibility assessment
  3. Potential scale of impact if implemented
  4. Alignment with real-world needs or problems
  5. Sustainability and long-term viability

Each idea should be evaluated by both an LLM and by human raters (2 per idea) who are blind to the experimental condition. The final score should be an average of these ratings.

Data Collection

Collect the following data for each idea:
1. Full text of the idea
2. Condition under which it was generated
3. Time spent on idea generation
4. Number and content of feedback instances received
5. Originality and impact scores (LLM and human)
6. User satisfaction with the idea generation process (post-task survey)
7. For the Integrated Condition, record all facilitator interventions based on dynamic feedback

Analysis Plan

Implement statistical analysis to compare the three conditions:
1. Calculate mean and standard deviation of originality and impact scores for each condition
2. Perform ANOVA to test for significant differences between conditions
3. If significant, conduct post-hoc tests to determine which specific conditions differ
4. Calculate effect sizes to quantify the magnitude of differences
5. Analyze the correlation between feedback quantity/quality and idea scores
6. Compare user satisfaction across conditions

Reporting

Generate a comprehensive report including:
1. Summary statistics for all metrics by condition
2. Statistical test results with p-values and effect sizes
3. Visualizations of score distributions across conditions
4. Sample ideas from each condition with their scores
5. Analysis of the relationship between feedback characteristics and idea quality
6. Recommendations for optimal feedback integration based on results

Experiment Flow

  1. Randomly assign participants to one of the three conditions
  2. Provide standardized instructions for idea generation
  3. Have participants generate ideas using the Scideator tool with the appropriate feedback mechanism(s) for their condition
  4. Collect all ideas and associated data
  5. Have ideas evaluated for originality and impact
  6. Analyze results and generate report

Please run the MINI_PILOT first, then if everything looks good, proceed to the PILOT. After the PILOT is complete, stop and do not run the FULL_EXPERIMENT until human verification of the results has occurred.

The experiment should be designed to minimize confounding variables by ensuring that all conditions have the same time constraints, instructions, and evaluation criteria, differing only in the feedback mechanisms provided.
